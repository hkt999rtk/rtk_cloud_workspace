# RTK Video Cloud STRIDE Matrix

- Classification: supporting-note
- Status: draft; static review, pending environment qualification
- Owner: RTK Cloud security analysis; component owners below are proposed assignments
- Last reviewed: 2026-10-05
- Applies to: the current-checkout Video Cloud model and its Account Manager,
  Admin, PKI, deployment, storage and recovery boundaries.

This matrix supports the [main threat model](../threat_models/rtk_video_cloud-stride-threat-model.md).
The main model records the inspected repository snapshot and scope. These are
abuse scenarios and review priorities, not confirmed exploitable findings or
live-environment sign-off. Original threat IDs remain stable.

Evidence labels distinguish **D** (documented requirement or design, retaining
the source's status), **I** (implementation inspected), **T** (test source
inspected, not executed), and **E** (dated environment execution evidence).
No E evidence was collected for this review. A D or I control does not establish
deployment, and a T reference does not claim a passing test. Unmentioned tests
have not been assessed. Deployment flags and residual-risk acceptance remain
open until the selected environment supplies evidence.

## Risk Calibration

Likelihood includes prerequisites and inspected controls: Low needs privileged
compromise or an unconfirmed exposure; Medium is a plausible authenticated-user,
stolen-credential or reachable configuration failure. Impact is High for
sensitive media, cross-device/tenant authority or critical availability loss;
Medium for bounded integrity or audit loss. Priority is a qualitative review
order, not a CVSS score. Critical is conditional on usable, broad signing or
administrative authority, not the presence of any secret. Disabled optional
paths do not carry the same exposure as enabled paths.

| ID | Boundary / STRIDE | Attacker prerequisite and abuse path | Likelihood rationale | Impact rationale | Priority | Proposed accountable component |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | TB1/TB2; Spoofing | Holder of a stolen valid token, or caller reaching a legacy certificate-header path with both compatibility flags enabled, impersonates a Device/App. Token forgery additionally requires signer compromise or verifier failure. | Medium: tokens and compatibility routes are practical entry points; Product/App PKI checks constrain them. | High: victim device commands or sensitive media become accessible. | High | Video API/authentication |
| S2 | TB2/TB4; Spoofing, Elevation of Privilege | Authenticated MQTT client changes tenant/client identity, bypasses topic rewriting, reaches reserved topics or another same-cloud device; alternatively, a caller exploits an exposed broker-auth callback/listener. | Medium: an authenticated client can attempt this; signed identity and ordered ACLs reduce success. | High: cross-tenant/device message disclosure or command injection. | High | MQTT foundation / EMQX integration |
| S3 | TB2/TB5/TB6; Spoofing, Elevation of Privilege | Holder of a valid credential from another environment, product or trust domain, or access to an overbroad signer, presents it to the wrong verifier/issuer. | Medium: identity domains and migration modes must compose correctly; broad signer access is an additional privileged prerequisite. | High: unauthorized Device/App/Internal Service identity; potentially fleet-wide authority with a usable broad signer. | High; Critical only for demonstrated broad signing/admin authority | PKI and workload identity |
| T1 | TB3/TB4; Tampering, Elevation of Privilege | Compromised service credential, authorized caller or replay-capable delivery path submits conflicting lifecycle operations or stale entitlement snapshots, corrupting ownership/options or runtime projections. | Medium: retries and concurrent changes are routine; receiver conflict checks reduce risk. | High: incorrect ownership or grants cross the control/runtime boundary. | High | Account Manager lifecycle integration, with Video entitlement receiver |
| T2 | TB4/TB5; Tampering | Holder of object-write or release-publishing credentials overwrites media or substitutes firmware/release payloads and their colocated checksums. | Low: requires write/publishing authority; hashes alone cannot prove provenance against that actor. | Media: bounded loss of recording integrity, Medium. Firmware/release: malicious code delivered to devices/workloads, High. | Media Medium; firmware/release High | Storage delivery and release pipeline; firmware consumer owner validates execution trust |
| T3 | TB2/TB7; Tampering, Elevation of Privilege | Device or presigned-URL holder replays completion, changes descriptors/objects, uses an earlier activation epoch, or sends a late PUT after failed verification/cleanup. | Medium: upload grants are transferable bearer capabilities; verifier/state controls reduce success. | High for unauthorized ready-media publication; Medium for bounded orphan-object/cost exposure, with shared-capacity exhaustion assessed under D1. | High | Video storage / clip verifier |
| T4 | TB5/TB6/TB9; Tampering, Elevation of Privilege | Attacker retains a credential revoked after the backup point, or gains recovery/archive influence; restoration rewinds revocation/ownership state and traffic resumes before reconciliation. | Medium: requires recovery plus stale-authority reuse or privileged recovery compromise. | High: revoked access, obsolete trust or old ownership becomes valid again. | High | Deployment recovery and PKI recovery |
| R1 | TB2/TB3/TB6/TB8/TB9; Repudiation | Legitimate or compromised actor performs issuance, provisioning, media access, webhook delivery or recovery whose audit trail omits actor, tenant, subject or outcome. | Medium: evidence crosses service and recovery boundaries. | Medium: investigation/accountability fail even when primary authorization holds. | Medium | Platform audit/observability, with emitting service owners |
| I1 | TB1/TB2/TB4; Information Disclosure | Holder of leaked URL/token, or user of device A, attempts device B's media through download compatibility, subscriber scope or token-bearing logs/referrers. | Medium: retained URL-token compatibility requires consistent binding/redaction. | High: private clips/snapshots escape device/tenant scope. | High | Video media API and logging |
| I2 | TB5/TB6/TB9; Information Disclosure, Elevation of Privilege | Actor with read access to Git, logs, CI artifacts, evidence or backups extracts still-usable storage/database/runtime/issuer credentials. | Medium: multiple copies and evidence producers exist; scope varies by credential. | High for usable production data/workload credentials; lower only when expiry, scope and data sensitivity demonstrably limit harm. Critical impact requires usable broad signing or administrative authority. | High baseline; conditional Critical as stated | Deployment SecretStore / CI and PKI custody |
| I3 | TB3/TB8; Information Disclosure, Tampering | When brand webhook delivery is enabled, tenant configuration access or event-routing manipulation sends another tenant's metadata to an attacker, targets internal services, or replays a captured signed event at the receiver. | Low while disabled; Medium when enabled with a configurable receiver. Public-address dialing and redirect refusal reduce SSRF likelihood. | Medium for minimal event metadata/replay; High if cross-tenant sensitive delivery or internal authority exposure is demonstrated. | Medium; conditional High | Brand webhook delivery and tenant integration |
| D1 | TB1/TB2/TB4/TB7/TB8; Denial of Service | Internet or authenticated client floods signaling, uploads, reconnects or retries; a tenant supplies slow/failing webhook endpoints. | Medium: exposed capacity can be consumed without signer compromise. | High: shared API/DB/Redis/EMQX/TURN/storage availability and cost are affected. | High | Video runtime capacity, with MQTT/TURN/storage operators |
| D2 | TB1/TB4/TB5; Denial of Service, Elevation of Privilege | External actor reaches unintended metrics, dashboard, database, NodePort or raw service listeners because of exposure drift. | Low pending proof of public reachability; private-listener policy reduces exposure. | High: control-plane disruption or privileged backend compromise. | Medium; High if an unauthenticated privileged surface is reachable | Deployment networking |
| E1 | TB1/TB2/TB3; Elevation of Privilege | Valid low-privilege `app`, `subscriber`, `device` or legacy `camera` credential reaches an admin-only route, wrong subject or overly broad compatibility alias. | Medium: route families and modes must preserve scope/subject rules. | High: unauthorized lifecycle changes or cross-device/tenant data access. | High | Video API authorization |
| E2 | TB3; Elevation of Privilege | Console/BFF user exploits stale local cache, local-session fallback or upstream partial failure to mutate authoritative tenant/device state. | Medium: cache and upstream failures are expected conditions. | High: unauthorized tenant/device changes if local state becomes authoritative. | High | Admin BFF, with Account Manager authorization |
| E3 | TB2/TB3/TB4/TB6; Elevation of Privilege | Previously authorized user/device keeps tokens, a WS/MQTT connection or established TURN/P2P media after grant removal, ownership change or certificate revocation. | Medium: revocation/refresh are normal flows; enforcement differs across transports and compatibility flags. | High: access survives the authority that originally allowed it. | High | Video authorization lifecycle, with MQTT/TURN and PKI consumers |

## Controls, Validation and Detection

The closure column specifies required evidence, not work completed in this
revision. Each owner should record the exact code/configuration, environment,
date and test outcome, or explicitly accept the residual risk. Proposed
detections also need deployment evidence before they count as controls.

| ID | Existing evidence and precise limit | Required validation / closure evidence | Proposed detection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | D: [auth contract][auth-contract] requires verified mTLS bootstrap and scoped runtime tokens. I: [router][router] `clientCertificateDeviceID`/`clientCertificateAppSubject` use TLS/registry checks in Product/App PKI modes. Non-PKI header compatibility remains behind `TrustedCertHeaders` + `EnableLegacyCert` + `X-Client-Verify: SUCCESS`; this is a mode-specific contract discrepancy to resolve. | Negative tests for forged headers, mismatched cert/token/subject and enabled legacy aliases; selected-profile evidence of trusted-hop enforcement, header stripping and direct-backend reachability. Disable unsupported compatibility or document a reviewed contract exception. | Subject/certificate mismatch and compatibility-bootstrap rates; new token use after reported theft. |
| S2 | I: [MQTT auth][mqtt] `mqttClaimsAllowed` binds signed `BrandCloudID`, client ID and MQTT service; `mqttExternalACLWithLogger` denies internal prefix, `$aws/#` and unauthorized reserved Shadow topics, and restricts App device topics. Non-App general-topic policy is broader and needs an explicit device-sharing decision. T: [MQTT tests][mqtt-tests] inspect strict legacy-grant recheck, revision lease and same-cloud App ACL cases. | Broker tests for rewrite order, wildcard/retained-message behavior, tenant and same-cloud device isolation, internal-server credentials, callback auth and listener exposure. Decide intended non-App same-cloud general topics. Strict `MQTTEntitlementsRequired` reconciles revision-zero tokens; when off, legacy ACL remains a migration exception. | Denials by tenant/client/topic; reserved-topic attempts; accepted identity versus rewrite prefix mismatch; repeated role-suffix connections. |
| S3 | D: [Platform PKI][platform-pki] separates trust/custody roles. I: [router][router] calls `VerifyDevice`/`VerifyApp` with configured environment; [device][device-cert] and [App][app-cert] admission retain certificate provenance and invoke configured live verifiers. This does not qualify every Internal Service verifier, signer role or deployed trust bundle. | Cross-environment/product/domain negative tests at each Device/App/Internal Service verifier; expired/revoked/root-distrusted credential denial; issuer-role/signer-policy evidence; deployed trust-bundle inventory and rollover/revocation-consumer receipts. | Wrong-domain/environment/product failures; unexpected issuer/signing-role use; stale consumer trust/CRL state. |
| T1 | D: [broker retirement](../../docs/cross-service-broker-packaging.md) routes lifecycle coordination through explicit APIs and producer-owned outboxes where needed. I: [receiver][entitlement-api] `handleAccountManagerEntitlementSnapshot` applies Platform snapshots and returns conflicts on revision/identity disagreement. This does not prove all lifecycle APIs/outboxes have equivalent protection. | Repeat/reorder/conflicting-payload and concurrent ownership/grant tests; authenticated service identity; producer retry/dead-letter recovery and receiver idempotency evidence; fail-closed projection freshness across failure/restart. | Snapshot conflict counts, outbox age/dead letters, ownership/projection divergence and unexplained revision regressions. |
| T2 | D: [artifact governance][artifacts] requires manifest/source/checksum verification; [storage operations](../../docs/storage-credential-lifecycle.md) defines credential and media/OTA qualification. A mutable checksum beside a mutable object is not independent publisher authentication. T3 covers upload intake, not all later overwrites. | Separate media overwrite/version controls from firmware/release provenance: scoped write credentials and object version/digest binding, independently trusted firmware signatures where required, and deployment verification of approved manifest/image digest. Record target-consumer rejection of altered payloads. | Unexpected object version/overwrite, digest mismatch, unapproved publisher/release identity and rollback attempts. |
| T3 | I: [upload][upload] `verifyAsset` checks size/type/ownership/checksum, reads multipart content for a full digest, and `RunOnce` checks activation epoch under lifecycle lock before ready publication. T: [upload tests][upload-tests] inspect earlier-epoch rejection and late-PUT cleanup after signed-URL expiry. | Real S3-compatible provider evidence for signed headers/checksums, multipart resume/abort/replay and post-verification overwrite resistance; crash/lease-race and deactivation tests; cleanup after late PUT/expiration; only ready rows exposed. | Verification/ownership/checksum failure, `ACTIVATION_CHANGED`, stale lease, orphan-object and cleanup-backlog metrics. |
| T4 | D: [restore][restore] excludes derived caches/leases, separates escrow/audit, fences traffic and requires post-backup revocation/security reconciliation. These are requirements, not a demonstrated security recovery exercise. | Isolated drill: revoke cert/token/grant after backup, restore the older archive, reconcile current authority, verify old credentials denied before resume; validate issuer/database/workload-secret consistency and partial-failure recovery. Retain independent audit plus approval/fence/resume evidence. | Restore without reconciliation receipt, revived revoked fingerprints/grants, old secret versions, revision regression or traffic while fenced. |
| R1 | D: [auth][auth-contract], [Platform PKI][platform-pki], [Admin boundary][admin-boundary] and [restore][restore] describe actor/authority boundaries. I: [webhook worker][webhook-worker] records delivery receipts. End-to-end retention/correlation is unqualified. | Trace issuance, lifecycle change, media access, failed/successful webhook and restore through actor, tenant, subject, source, operation ID and outcome; verify redaction/access controls and independent audit retention across restore. | Missing correlation/actor/tenant fields, privileged actions without upstream authorization, audit lag and retention gaps. |
| I1 | D: [auth route matrix][auth-contract] limits subscriber scope and records legacy download query-token compatibility. This does not establish redaction in every proxy, service, support export or receiver. | Cross-device/tenant download tests for accepted scopes and snapshot paths; inspect sanitized proxy/application traces; demonstrate token-free referrer/evidence handling and limited download-capability lifetime. | Cross-subject denial, abnormal download volume and secret-safe token-pattern scanning of logs/exports. |
| I2 | D: [secret governance][secrets] distinguishes current SecretStore authority, Kubernetes synchronized copies and OpenBao issuer state/target KV cutover. [Restore][restore] separates escrow from archives. Per-secret scope, validity and reachability determine impact. | Secret scanning of tracked files and release/evidence packages with a maintained tool; reader/writer/scope inventory; rotation/revocation and copy-reconciliation exercise. Broad signer/admin exposure requires containment and requalification; a scoped storage key does not automatically imply platform compromise. | Scanner findings, unexpected secret/issuer reads, stale copy versions and old-credential use after rotation. |
| I3 | I: [transport][webhook-transport] dials resolved public addresses, disables proxy selection, requires 443 and refuses redirects; [worker][webhook-worker] validates subscriptions, signs timestamp/body and records receipts. T: [worker tests][webhook-tests] inspect minimal signed-event/receipt assertions. HMAC alone does not stop replay at a receiver. | When enabled: tenant routing and transfer/in-flight generation tests, metadata minimization, DNS rebinding/mixed public-private answers/redirect tests, receiver timestamp window + event-ID deduplication, secret rotation and disable/delete evidence. Do not infer these controls for legacy `internal/notify` or Azure Event Hubs. | Tenant-route mismatch, blocked destination/redirect, signature/replay rejection, repeated delivery, stale generation and dead-letter volume. |
| D1 | D: [streaming][streaming] defines lifecycle/timeout behavior. I: [upload][upload] bounds descriptors/verification state; [webhook worker][webhook-worker] bounds batches/attempts. Individual bounds do not prove aggregate capacity or tenant fairness. | Chosen-scale overload tests for tenant/device quotas, body/SDP/object limits, reconnect load, TURN allocation limits, queue backpressure and timeout cleanup; measure revocation-check load and dependency-failure behavior. | Pending sessions, allocation/queue age, DB/Redis latency, broker reconnect spikes, object growth/cost and retry exhaustion. |
| D2 | D: [LKE edge](../../docs/lke-external-haproxy-edge.md) limits public edge ports and requires private exporters; this is profile policy plus dated historical evidence, not today's reachability result. | Selected-environment external/internal exposure inventory, NodePort/firewall allowlists, authenticated admin listeners and backend-bypass tests; preserve dated sanitized results. | Exposure/config drift and unsolicited requests to internal/admin listeners. |
| E1 | D: [auth route matrix][auth-contract] declares accepted scope/subject combinations. I: [router][router] contains multiple compatibility adapters; exhaustive route parity was not established here. | Route-by-route negative scope/subject/tenant tests for modern/legacy aliases, admin versus internal-service authority, disabled optional routes and malformed/duplicate credentials; reviewed default-deny inventory. | Unexpected successful privileged requests by low-privilege actors; route/scope denial patterns. |
| E2 | D: [Admin boundary][admin-boundary] requires upstream authority and non-authoritative caches. I: Video's [Account Manager authorizer](../../repos/rtk_video_cloud/internal/httpapi/account_manager_authorizer.go) denies upstream errors/non-2xx; this does not qualify the separate Admin BFF implementation. | BFF mutation tests with missing/expired upstream session, stale cache, timeout, denial and partial response; trace accepted mutations to current upstream authorization and authoritative state. | Mutations without upstream authorization, local-session fallback and stale-cache writes. |
| E3 | I: [auth][auth-code] `Service.Refresh` preserves signed options/revisions; configured certificate checks still apply. D: [architecture][architecture] records revision-aware HTTP Shadow, signaling/new TURN and WS checks, five-second WS polling and capped MQTT sessions. I/T: [MQTT auth][mqtt] and [tests][mqtt-tests] reconcile even revision-zero device tokens when `MQTTEntitlementsRequired` is enabled; otherwise legacy exceptions remain. Existing TURN allocations/P2P media are not proven evicted. | Per-channel revocation matrix: issuance/refresh, HTTP routes, WS admission/frame/idle, MQTT reconnect/established lease, new TURN and existing TURN/P2P; include cert revoke, grant removal and ownership transfer. Measure end-to-end invalidation under load/outage and stale caches. Prove active-media teardown, or explicitly accept a bounded residual lifetime. | Accepted stale revisions, post-revoke media/commands, sessions beyond lease, polling lag and failed invalidation delivery. |
